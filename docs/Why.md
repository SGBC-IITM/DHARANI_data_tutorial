## 3d Histology of the brain
The ability to visualize and analyze whole organs in their native 3D context, with high resolution, offers unprecedented opportunities for studying complex structures such as the brain and understanding the intricate relationships between cells and tissues at an organ-wide scale. The increasing availability of publicly accessible 3D histology datasets is a crucial driver of progress in this field, fostering collaboration among researchers worldwide and accelerating the pace of scientific discovery.

For the study of the developing human brain, three-dimensional (3D) histology offers significant advantages over traditional two-dimensional (2D) methods. By reconstructing serial histological sections into a 3D volume, researchers can visualize the intricate architecture of the brain in its entirety, akin to magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). This volumetric perspective allows for a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of the growth and development of different brain regions and their layers throughout gestation, which is particularly crucial during the rapidly changing second trimester. Unlike 2D slices, 3D histology captures the spatial context necessary to understand the complex organization of neural circuits and to study developmental processes that occur across multiple planes. Furthermore, the detailed cellular resolution provided by histology, when extended to three dimensions, enables the investigation of microstructural changes and the identification of cytoarchitectural boundaries with greater precision than macroscopic imaging techniques. The ability to explore the developing brain in 3D through resources like DHARANI provides an invaluable tool for advancing our understanding of human neurodevelopment.

## Dharani online platform
Dharani's online platform is dedicated to providing three-dimensional (3D) histological reconstructions of the developing human brain during the critical second trimester of gestation, specifically spanning from 14 to 24 gestational weeks (GW). This unique resource encompasses five fetal brains and offers a wealth of detailed anatomical information. The platform features over 5000 histological sections, stained with both Nissl and hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), at a resolution of 20 µm for coronal and sagittal sections. In addition to these high-resolution histological data, DHARANI also includes postmortem MRI scans and a comprehensive neuroanatomical atlas containing 466 annotated sections that cover approximately 500 distinct brain structures. The pixel resolution of the annotated histological sections is remarkably high, rendered at 1 µm per pixel. A key advancement offered by DHARANI is the 3D reconstruction of these histological sections, which enables a volumetric view of the developing fetal brain, allowing for visualization in all three anatomical planes—axial, coronal, and sagittal—much like MRI.
