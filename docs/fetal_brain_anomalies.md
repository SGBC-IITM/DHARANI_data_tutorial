# Fetal brain anomalies
Many fetal brain abnormalities can be studied using histological methods, especially those affecting cellular organization, cortical development, vascularization, and response to injury. Below are key fetal brain abnormalities that can be investigated using various histology techniques:


## Neuronal Migration & Cortical Development Disorders

These disorders arise due to defective neuroblast migration, leading to abnormal cortical layering and structure.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Lissencephaly (Smooth Brain) | * Thickened cortical plate with loss of normal lamination.<br> * Reduced or absent gyri and sulci.<br>* Increased heterotopic neurons in white matter.<br><br> - Kuchelmeister, K., Bergmann, M. & Gullotta, F. Neuropathology of lissencephalies. Child's Nerv Syst 9, 394–399 (1993). https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00306191| Nissl (for cortical layering), NeuN (neurons), MAP2 (dendrites).|
| Polymicrogyria | * Excessively small and irregular gyri.<br> * Fusion of cortical layers and disorganized neurons.<br><br> - Squier, W., Jansen, A. Polymicrogyria: pathology, fetal origins and mechanisms. acta neuropathol commun 2, 80 (2014). https://doi.org/10.1186/s40478-014-0080-3| HE, Nissl, NeuN, GFAP (glial scarring).|
| Heterotopias (Neuronal Misplacement) |* Ectopic clusters of neurons in white matter.<br> *Loss of normal cortical layering.| NeuN, Nissl, Doublecortin (DCX, for migrating neurons).|
| Schizencephaly (Cleft Brain) | * Gray matter-lined clefts extending from ventricles to pial surface.<br> *Neuronal loss and gliosis in cleft walls.<br><br> - Griffiths, P.D. Schizencephaly revisited. Neuroradiology 60, 945–960 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1007/s00234-018-2056-7| NeuN, GFAP (gliosis), MAP2.|


## Proliferation & Growth Disorders
These conditions result from abnormal progenitor cell proliferation and differentiation.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Microcephaly |* Reduced cortical thickness.<br> * Simplified cortical folding. <br>* Decreased neuronal density.| Ki67 (proliferation marker), NeuN, Nissl.|
|Megalencephaly | * Enlarged brain with excessive neuronal growth.<br> *Enlarged neurons with abnormal morphology.| Nissl, NeuN, Ki67.|
| Tuberous Sclerosis | * Dysplastic giant neurons (abnormally large, balloon-like cells).<br> *Cortical tubers with disrupted lamination. | NeuN, GFAP, TSC1/TSC2 mutations. |

## Vascular & Hypoxic-Ischemic Lesions
Oxygen deprivation or vascular abnormalities can cause severe fetal brain injury.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Germinal Matrix Hemorrhage (GMH) | * Hemorrhage in the germinal matrix near ventricles.<br> *Periventricular gliosis and neuron loss.| HE (for hemorrhage), GFAP (astrocytes), Iba1 (microglia).|
| Periventricular Leukomalacia (PVL) | * Necrotic white matter with cystic spaces.<br> * Increased microglial activation. | Iba1 (microglia), MBP (myelin loss), GFAP. |
| Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE) | * Shrinkage and eosinophilia of neurons ("red neurons").<br> *Gliosis and white matter injury. | HE, GFAP, Iba1, TUNEL (for apoptosis).|

## Myelination & White Matter Disorders
Myelin defects affect connectivity and neural communication.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Delayed or Abnormal Myelination | * Reduced or absent myelin staining in expected regions.<br> * Thinned corpus callosum. | MBP (myelin), Luxol Fast Blue (LFB).|
| Leukodystrophies | * Loss of white matter integrity.<br> * Vacuolization and gliosis.| MBP, GFAP, PAS (for metabolic storage disorders). |


## Developmental Brain Infections (TORCH Infections)
Maternal infections during pregnancy can cause severe brain damage.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Infection | * Periventricular calcifications. <br> * Enlarged cells with intranuclear inclusions. | HE, IHC for CMV antigens.|
| Toxoplasmosis | * Cyst-like structures with bradyzoites. <br> * Microglial nodules and gliosis.| HE, IHC for Toxoplasma gondii. | 
| Zika Virus Infection | * Severe microcephaly.<br> * Cortical thinning and gliosis.| NeuN, GFAP, viral RNA detection.|

## Genetic Syndromes & Structural Malformations
Certain genetic disorders cause specific histological abnormalities.
| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21)|* Decreased cortical folding complexity.<br> * Delayed dendritic and synaptic maturation.| NeuN, MAP2, β-amyloid (early Alzheimer's pathology).|
| Holoprosencephaly | * Incomplete separation of brain hemispheres.<br> * Midline fusion of structures.| HE, Nissl.|

## Tumor-Like Growths & Malformations
Fetal brain tumors are rare but can be identified histologically.

| Condition | Histology findings | Stains/Markers |
|:-- | :-- | :-- |
| Congenital Neuroepithelial Tumors |* Abnormal proliferation of neuroepithelial cells <br> * Rosette-like structures.| Ki67 (proliferation), NeuN, GFAP.|


Histology provides a powerful way to study fetal brain abnormalities by allowing visualization of cellular structures, neuronal organization, vascular integrity, and pathological lesions. Immunohistochemistry and special stains further enhance the ability to identify specific abnormalities.
